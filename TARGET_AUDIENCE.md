# Target audience

Root Quest and *The Fossil Record of English* are for **educated adults who are native English speakers** and never took the time to learn Latin and Greek roots.

## What that means

- **They already know the words.** They use *portable*, *biography* and *telescope* without thinking. Nothing teaches them what those words mean. What's new is the root inside each word and the connections between words that share it.
- **They read widely and meet hard words.** The payoff is a better guess at *obloquy*, *pusillanimous* or *tergiversate* when one turns up in a book, and better recall after looking one up.
- **They can handle real etymology.** Source words (*cadere*, *casus*), stem variants (*cad / cas / cid*), assimilated prefixes (*ad-* → *ac-*, *af-*, *al-*), native English cognates (*ped-* / *foot*), and false friends (*pediatric* is from Greek *paid-*, "child") are all fair game. Explain them plainly. Don't leave them out.
- **Their time is limited, and they won't tolerate being talked down to.** Short sessions and spaced review suit them. Childish copy, cheerleading and trivially easy questions don't.

## Implications for the app

- **Grade-school roots stay, but go fast.** Familiar roots (*bio*, *tele*, *photo*, *port*, *spect*…) are covered quickly: one confirming question each, with no lengthy introduction. Roots answered correctly are scheduled far out. The bulk of study time goes to less obvious roots and hard words.
- **Questions should require thought.** Infer a rare word's meaning from its parts, find the root shared by several words, or spot the word that only looks related. Distractors should be plausible.
- **Accuracy matters more than neatness.** Never invent an etymology to make a connection tidy. When a word's modern meaning has drifted from its parts, say so.
- **Tone:** curious, precise, lightly witty. Like the book, not a children's game.
