# 7. Keep Looking

Eventually, some of the connections that once needed your full attention may become familiar. You notice them without stopping to reconstruct them.

That is a satisfying change. You have returned to something often enough that it has begun to feel like part of what you know.

What happens next can be quite ordinary. You read an article. A word catches your attention. You recognize a piece, or you wonder whether you do. You look it up, keep reading, and perhaps mention the discovery to someone later.

Progress can be quiet. A paragraph becomes easier to follow. A word that once made you hesitate barely slows you down. You may notice the change only when you return to something you read months ago.

## Notice what has changed

You might find that you remember a definition because it now belongs to a family. You might notice a relationship between two words you have used for years. You might become more comfortable looking something up because you have a specific question to ask.

You might also discover that one subject interests you more than you expected. A word about sound leads you toward music; a word about earth leads you toward a process you want to understand. Follow the interest when you have time.

There is no requirement to expand your collection endlessly. You can spend longer with these roots, let reading bring new ones to your attention, or simply enjoy the connections that stay with you.

A small practice has done useful work if it leaves you more willing to engage with the language in front of you.

## Begin wherever you are

Choose something you want to read. Let a familiar root catch your attention, or follow an unfamiliar word far enough to learn something about it.

You can begin in the middle of a page, with the vocabulary you have today.

Somewhere in an ordinary sentence, something familiar will turn out to have another feature you had not noticed. It may be a small discovery. You may pause for only a moment.

Then you will go on reading, with a little more to see.
